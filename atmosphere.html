at.lattice.css

Eight paths, running at once

Every cell below is a real frame with its own data-lattice, alive right now. The sheet contains no rule that mentions this gallery, this section, or this page — only a rule about frames, tracks and views. Each path is one way of tying a frame's views together and moving between them; it traverses whichever frame carries it, and the page's own frame is not special.

Control and App differ in one clause: a Control tile divides a glass that never moves, an App panel is a fixed-size tile on a wall that does. Both keep their own overflow — a panel is a frame, and a frame keeps what does not fit.

Those set the path through this page — the same eight rules, applied to the root frame. The gallery keeps its own the whole time.

at.layer.css

On the plane, or above it

What stays in place, floats, or appears on demand? Pick a projection, then open the Guide. Its identity and paged path remain the same. These Layer buttons request the projection for both panels; the CSS contract can override Guide and Axes independently. Embedded relationships keep their own layout slots.

A level is a relationship, so Layer states both ends of it. While a projection is open the page falls back exactly as far as the projection came forward — --page-far is --layer-z pointing the other way. Nothing downstream names a projection, so a fourth one added tomorrow recedes the page today.

The footer is a frame too, and it names the edge it belongs to: data-edge="block-end". The projections that hang furniture off the window — the drawer and the top menu — lift it into a drawer on that edge; a pop-over, which belongs to a trigger, leaves it on the plane. Its own centred layout goes with it either way, because every plane carries its own.

Lattice × Layer inline edge drawer pop-over
continuous an article an article with a nav drawer an article with margin notes
app a fixed workspace a shelf app a workspace with inspectors
control a wall board a console with a drawer a console with detail cards
pages a bound book a book with an index a book with footnotes

These combinations illustrate how presence and traversal can compose. Their responsibilities remain distinct; their dependencies must be made explicit.

at.look.css

Thirty-one looks, one skeleton

A look declares colour, typography, texture and the air between planes. It moves nothing and places nothing. It also declares how wide a character is in the face it just chose — --face-aspect — which is what lets every component solve its own type size in a box a look just made narrower.

The measure contract

Same word, same box. The only difference is whether the box was told how long the word would be in the face the look just picked. Switch looks above and watch the left one hold while the right one breaks.

This is why a component can solve its own type size at all. It knows the worst case it must hold, in characters; the look knows how wide a character is; the box knows how wide it is. Three numbers, one min(), and no breakpoint anywhere.

Density and size are supporting controls, orthogonal to all six: --density-scale is the reader's air, --lattice-scale is what a path needs, and they multiply.

at.life.css

Motion is a score, not a property

Life animates roles, never elements. The markup says a thing is a surface, a select, a piece of reading, the masthead or a voice; the score decides what those feel like. Nothing on this page carries an animation.

Scroll-linked, and scored against the frame's own --flow timeline.

crossing How a projection arrives

The flow names the manner; Layer supplies the distance and the hinge, so one crossing reads at drawer scale and at pop-over scale alike. Pick a projection above and open a panel to feel it.

reduced motion Honoured, not overridden

Every score collapses to its still form under prefers-reduced-motion. Pointer feedback stays, because that is information rather than decoration.

Where a declaration is filed

File a declaration with the decision it serves. The same CSS property can serve different decisions in different contexts; the table below describes this implementation's conventions. Share facts through tokens instead of competing overrides.

Sheet Its space Files
Layout The flat plane, X by Y display: grid | flex, grid-*, flex-*, gap, width, height, min-*, max-*, aspect-ratio, margin, padding, columns
Layer The stack of planes, along z z-index, isolation, position: fixed | absolute | sticky, [popover], ::backdrop, <dialog>, elevation shadow, backdrop-filter, mix-blend-mode, opacity
Lattice Traversal across the stack overflow, scroll-snap-*, scroll() and view() timelines, view-transition-name, perspective, perspective-origin, transform-style, :focus-visible order
Look Surfaces and materials color, background-color, border, border-radius, box-shadow, filter, font-*, text-align, line-height
Life Change over time transition, animation, :hover, :active, :focus, pointer-events
Lens Experience policy Nothing of its own: seeds, defaults and timing for the other five
at.components.css A part's own shape

Intrinsic padding, borders, and the box a part needs to hold its worst-case content. Knows nothing about looks or paths.

at.local.css Quantity at the call site

How many columns, how much gap, which plane. Named steps on scales the axes own — data-gap="open", never a raw length.

the razor How to tell a local from a hardcode

If changing data-look or data-density does not move it, it is not a local. It is a hardcode, and it belongs nowhere.

the markup Declares a semantic contract

An ID addresses an instance; a class names a kind. data-place names responsibility, data-part its constituents, and data-role a shared presentation responsibility.

One component, five owners

A tab set nobody owns outright

Tabs are usually shipped as a component with variants. Here the same markup is filed like everything else: each sheet writes its own slice and none of them knows the others' answers. Move the list with data-tabs, then change a Look or a Life — the tab set follows without being told.

What exists

A tablist, six tabs and six panels, paired by aria-controls. No class or attribute in the markup says top, side, selected colour or animation — so no later choice has to undo one.

role="tablist" role="tab" role="tabpanel" aria-controls data-role="select"
at.components.css — the inside

The set is a grid with two named areas, list and panels, and the parts are given their padding and a minimum panel height. It names the areas and stops: where they go is not its question.

.tab-set grid-area: list grid-area: panels --fit-chars
at.layout.css — where the list sits

classic runs the list along the top with the panel below; column stacks it at the left with the panel beside it. A column needs a column to live in, so under 26rem of panel width it falls back to classic on its own.

data-tabs grid-template-areas @container panel 26rem
at.look.css — without naming a tab

No look contains a tab rule. A tab's border is currentColor at the look's stroke strength; the panel's corners are the look's radius and its padding scales with the look's density — so thirty-one looks restyle the set and none of them had to be taught it exists.

currentColor --stroke-strength --radius-lg --look-density --look-leading
at.life.css — how the swap feels

The selected tab is scored by the current Life: pulse lifts it, wiggle bounces it, glitch flickers it. A panel that has just appeared arrives on the look's own entrance, and still switches both off.

data-life [aria-selected="true"] --stagger-anim --enter-time
atmosphere.js — which one is chosen

Selection is state, so it is the script's and nothing else is. It keeps aria-selected, the roving tabindex and each panel's hidden in step, and moves between tabs on the arrow keys, Home and End.

aria-selected tabindex hidden Arrow / Home / End

Addressable

Every composition is in the URL. #at=… is the compact form; the long named form stays readable forever, and old links canonicalize.

Progressive

Without script the page keeps its complete inline structure. Projections are an enhancement; a control for a missing capability stays hidden.

Operable

A paced frame is focusable and answers arrows, Page keys and Home / End. Tabs are the full ARIA pattern. Nothing is mouse-only.

Printable

Whatever the six say on screen, print returns one complete paged document with every frame flattened and in order.
